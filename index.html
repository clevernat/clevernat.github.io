The review message + 8 steps

The follow-up message I sent after every first draft in the 5-hours-a-week test: Claude checks its own work against the client's order, lists anything it made up, and sends a corrected file. Plus the exact 8 steps I used for all 13 orders. No income promises: the clients were made up, and finding real ones is the real job.

Download the prompt View it Watch the full tutorial
  1. Open claude.ai, start a new chat and click the ghost icon for an incognito chat.
  2. Click +, choose Add files, and attach the client's files.
  3. Paste the client's order and add "Deliver it as a downloadable file." Send, then download the first draft.
  4. Paste the review message from the file and send it. Download the corrected version.
  5. Read every word yourself before it goes to the client. The review usually fixes mistakes, but not always.
Do this on a laptop or desktop at claude.ai, where uploading and downloading files is easiest.

First-client prompt

One copy-paste prompt that turns two screenshots of a local business website into a real problem, a fixed-price offer, a short cold email, follow-ups and honest replies. When something is not decided yet, like how you get paid, Claude writes [ASK ME] instead of making it up. No income promises: nobody can guarantee you replies.

Download the prompt View it Watch the full tutorial
  1. Pick one local business you could genuinely help, and take two screenshots of its website: the whole page on your computer, and the first screen on your phone.
  2. Open claude.ai, start a new chat, and attach both screenshots.
  3. Paste the prompt and fill in the [brackets]: your skill, your name, your price, your mailing address and the business.
  4. Send. Then open the real website and check every claim yourself, because Claude only sees your screenshots.
  5. Ask Claude to make the free before-and-after mockup, then send the email from your own inbox. Keep your address and the "reply no" line in it.
Do this on a laptop or desktop at claude.ai. In the US, business cold emails fall under CAN-SPAM: include a real postal address and an easy way to opt out. Not legal advice.
