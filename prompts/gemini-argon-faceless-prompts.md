# Gemini 4 Argon faceless channel: every prompt + the answer key

From the AI Walkthru video "I Tried Starting a Faceless Channel With ONLY Gemini 4 Argon". Tested on a Google AI Pro account on October 20, 2026. Gemini 4 Argon needs Google AI Pro or Ultra. Plans, limits and menus change.

Swap "Margin Story" and the Costco topic for your own channel and video.

## 1. Pick the model

Model picker → 4 Argon (complex tasks). Use 3.8 Flash for images and small jobs.

## 3. Find a niche that pays

Argon, Chat mode

```
I want to start a faceless YouTube channel in 2026, as one person with no face on camera. Give me the top 10 faceless niches with the highest RPM (revenue per 1,000 views) in a table: niche, typical RPM range in US dollars, why advertisers pay well, and one example of a faceless format that works. Then rank the 3 best for a beginner. Cite a source for every RPM range, and say clearly when a number is only an estimate.
```

## 4. Prove it with real channels

Do NOT use the @ menu: it only offers YouTube Music. Check every count on the channel page yourself.

```

Give each channel's link, its real subscriber count today, and its start date, and tell me which numbers you could not check.
```

## The answer key I used

Each step passes only if the check holds on the real output. Kept on camera either way.

1. Niches (RPM table): every RPM range has a named source; finance/investing, insurance or tech appear in the top 3; any range quoted without a source or above about 40 USD is flagged as an estimate.
2. Proof channels: every channel listed exists on YouTube; open one and its subscriber count is within about 10% of what Argon said; it really is faceless.
3. Video ideas: at least one of the three "top demand" ideas shows real YouTube search interest in Google Trends (YouTube Search, past 12 months, US).
4. Character: matches the description (one mascot, readable at thumbnail size); a second image made with the saved Style keeps the same look (face, outfit, colours).
5. Banner: YouTube banner is 2560 x 1440 with text and logo inside the 1546 x 423 safe area; profile picture works as a circle at 98 px. Check the downloaded file's pixels.
6. Script: every number has a source; the Costco rotisserie-chicken numbers (chickens sold per year, the $4.99 price, any "loses $X million a year" claim, membership-fee revenue) are traced to Costco's own reports or a named primary source and marked correct, outdated or estimate. (Changed after step 1: on the recorded run Argon ranked business-history documentaries as a beginner pick and suggested the Costco chicken topic itself; finance how-to is YMYL.)
7. Video: the generated video/scene shows the same mascot and says the same facts as the checked script.
8. Thumbnail: 16:9, 4 words or fewer, the mascot readable at phone size.
9. Upload pack: title at most 70 characters, description with the AI disclosure line, chapters start at 0:00.
10. Weekly routine: a Scheduled task exists with its next run time shown.
11. Disclosure: Verify AI finds the SynthID watermark in the thumbnail, and YouTube's altered/synthetic content setting is named.

## The 8 mistakes

1. Running everything on Argon. My 5-hour limit hit 100% about an hour after the first Argon prompt. Use Flash for small jobs.
2. Trusting an RPM table. Argon's ranges are estimates; a 2026 study of real channels found a business and finance median of about $2 per 1,000 views.
3. Trusting subscriber counts from a chat. One channel did not exist, one handle was wrong, and most counts were far off.
4. Searching full questions in Google Trends. Shorten them to 2–3 words.
5. Checking the preview instead of the file. The banner came out 2752 x 1536 with the mascot outside the safe area.
6. A fact-check that doesn't search. Check the original source (here: Costco's 10-K and shareholder slides).
7. Slideshows with no story. YouTube's inauthentic-content rule (July 2025) names image slideshows with minimal or no narrative.
8. Expecting automation to run the channel. A scheduled action gives you ideas; you still check, record and upload.
